    How to use Sheets Lab

    Sheets Lab teaches Google Sheets formulas from scratch in 5 days, about an hour a day. You learn by doing: each lesson explains one idea, then gives you small exercises that are checked straight away.

    What's on the screen

    Top barThe list of lessons, your progress, this help page (?) and light or dark mode (◐)
    Lesson, on the leftThe explanation first, then "Your turn" with the exercises
    Practice workbook, on the rightA mini Google Sheet with made-up data. On a phone, open it with the Show workbook button.

    How each lesson works

    1. Read the lesson. It explains one idea with a small example.
    2. Press a ▶ example button to run that formula in the practice workbook. The cells it fills turn green.
    3. Do the exercises under "Your turn". Each one tells you which workbook tab and which cell your formula is for. Type your formula in the box, starting with =, and press Check.
    4. Stuck? Press Hint. Still stuck? Press Show answer, then type it in yourself.
    5. Tasks marked "In Google Sheets" are done in your own Google Sheet, which you set up in lesson 5. Tick Done in my sheet when you've finished.

    Good to know

    • You don't have to type cell addresses. While you're typing a formula, click a cell in the workbook to add it. Click one cell, then hold Shift and click another, to add a range like B2:B15.
    • Any formula that gives the right answer counts, even if it's different from ours.
    • Your progress is saved in this browser on this device. A different browser or device, or clearing your browsing data, starts you from zero.
    • For the Google Sheets tasks you need a free Google account.

    Words you'll see

    CellOne box in the sheet. Its address is its column letter and row number, like B2.
    RangeA block of cells, written first cell, colon, last cell. B2:B15 is column B from row 2 to row 15.
    TabOne page of the spreadsheet. The practice workbook has tabs like registrations and zoom.
    FormulaAn instruction that starts with =, like =B2+C2. The cell shows its answer.
    FunctionA ready-made calculation with a name, like SUM or COUNTIF.
    Practice workbookA mini Google Sheet with made-up data. Click a tab name to switch pages.
    Green cells show the answer to your last formula.
